Exploratory stage · 29 September 2026 · recorded citizen care

Berlin watering patterns and weather

Warmer months coincide with more recorded events and more distinct trees watered after broad seasonal and yearly adjustment. Previous-month temperature is much less informative in this initial monthly analysis. These findings do not establish the cause of the 2022 increase or a cooling effect of watering.

The agreed stage retains the three research questions provisionally. It describes monthly care, examines the weather-response question, and checks the feasibility of subsequent LST analysis. A null association is a valid result.

1. Monthly counts are ready

The panel contains 816 district-months and 36,856 LOR neighbourhood-months from May 2020 to December 2025. It reconciles to 57,786 geographically assigned events, 2,249,502 litres and 11,127 distinct trees. Events and litres exclude 688 source events (28,485 litres) that could not be assigned through the existing tree crosswalk.

Counts describe platform records. A recorded zero does not establish that no municipal, private or unlogged watering occurred. Rates use the current tree inventory. Distinct trees are counted within each period and must not be added across months to obtain annual distinct counts.

2. The 2022 peak needs a more precise explanation

2022 has the largest annual event and litre totals in this linked sample. However, the comparable June–September period has 13,838 events in 2020 and 12,660 in 2022. Distinct trees are also higher in that 2020 window (3,734 versus 3,354), whereas 2022 has more litres (604,930 versus 425,720). June 2021 also has more events than June 2022.

At Tempelhof, June–September 2022 has 21 days with maximum air temperature of at least 30 °C and less recorded rainfall than the other complete-rainfall windows shown. Its seasonal mean temperature is close to 2020 and 2021, and below 2023 and 2024. Thus a single annual count or average temperature cannot explain participation. Campaigns, platform use, repeated care and recording changes remain plausible alternatives.

3. Initial weather associations

We downloaded the public historical archive for DWD Berlin-Tempelhof, station 00433. This provides air temperature and precipitation as shared temporal context for Berlin. It does not provide district-specific heat or Landsat land-surface temperature.

Separate models relate log(1 + monthly activity) to temperature and rainfall in either the same month or the preceding calendar month. Each includes calendar-month and year indicators. There are 60 eligible complete calendar months in 2021–2025; two incomplete rainfall months leave 58 observations per model. Approximate uncertainty intervals use three-month HAC errors with actual calendar gaps preserved.

  • Same-month heat alignment: the event-count slope is 0.817 log(1 + events) units per 5 °C (approximate 95% interval 0.142 to 1.492). Distinct-tree counts have a similar positive estimate. The litre estimate is positive but less precise.
  • Previous-month heat: the event-count slope is 0.103 (−0.515 to 0.722). All three previous-month temperature intervals cross zero. This does not rule out a response operating over days rather than a full calendar month.
  • Rainfall: these specifications do not give precise adjusted evidence of a rainfall association. Rainfall is not a complete measure of soil or tree water stress.
  • Excluding 2022: the positive same-month temperature alignment in events and distinct trees remains. The pattern therefore does not depend solely on retaining that year.

These are retrospective exploratory comparisons with several outcomes and specifications. The intervals are approximate and not adjusted for multiple comparisons. The log(1 + activity) slopes are not exact percentage changes in arithmetic mean activity. Broad seasonal adjustment does not remove all confounding, platform growth or changing logging. Same-month data cannot establish whether heat preceded watering within the month.

4. Socioeconomic context

Across the 60 complete months, middle-status neighbourhoods average 0.987 recorded events per 1,000 current inventory trees per month, compared with 0.369 in high-status, 0.253 in low-status and 0.454 in very-low-status neighbourhoods. This is not a monotonic rich-to-poor gradient. Differences in street/park tree composition and the denominator also matter.

The MSS categories refer to the end of 2024 and classify neighbourhood context, not individual volunteers or historical district income. Seven LORs are unclassified and retained separately. This first comparison does not estimate whether vulnerability modifies the heat-response slope. That requires a specified repeated-neighbourhood model with shared-weather uncertainty.

5. Supplied monthly Landsat file: audit and provisional comparison

The supplied CSV is now incorporated. It contains 9,312 rows for 2018–2025, covering 96 distinct Ortsteil names. There are 1,811 missing LST values (19.4% of source rows). Missing temperatures are never replaced with zero.

Two source issues require explicit exclusions. Buckow has two different observations every month, without component IDs; neither is selected or averaged. Schlachtensee is absent. Berlin's official 2020 boundary change created Schlachtensee from parts of Nikolassee and Zehlendorf, so the latter two are excluded until the extraction geography is clarified.

Tree-inventory points were assigned directly to official ALKIS Ortsteil polygons. All 57,786 linked events and 2,249,502 litres reconcile without loss or district disagreements. The new table has 6,596 Ortsteil-months, including events, litres and monthly distinct trees for all 97 official areas. Temperature comparisons use 93 provisional areas; matching names and districts does not confirm identical source polygons.

LST_C_count remains undefined. Positive values range from 1 to 64,360 and are often repeated at an area's maximum, suggesting a spatial count. This is not enough to identify its exact meaning. The file also omits acquisition dates, sensor/product details, cloud masks, compositing rules and rainfall provenance. No count is interpreted as an independent satellite observation or used as an inferential weight.

For litres per hectare, the raw same-month Spearman correlation is +0.299, while the correlation between residual LST and residual log(1 + litres/ha), after removing Ortsteil and actual year-month means, is only +0.025. The two columns use different correlation definitions; the figure compares Pearson correlations using the same log exposure on both sides.

The next-month residual correlation for litres is −0.024, and becomes +0.005 when 2022 is excluded. Across all calendar months, that next-month correlation is +0.075. Thus the data do not provide a stable descriptive cooling pattern across these specifications. This is neither an estimated cooling effect nor evidence that watering has no physiological or local microclimatic benefit.

The previous-LST/current-watering residual correlation for litres is +0.057 in the main season. These local residual descriptions ask a different question from the Berlin-wide DWD response analysis: actual year-month adjustment removes shared city-wide temporal variation, including common heat episodes. They cannot disprove a city-wide heat response.

The relative-count screen retains LST counts at least half the area's observed maximum. It is a diagnostic restriction, not validated cloud coverage. No p-values, confidence intervals or causal effect estimates are reported for this provisional extension. Reported rainfall is preserved under an explicitly unverified source label and is not used as a physical adjustment variable.

What is needed to strengthen the interpretation

The next input is the extraction script or metadata defining the original polygons, both Buckow components, Landsat processing, LST_C_count and rainfall. These details determine whether the geographic alignment and temperature summaries can become substantive thesis evidence. The three research questions remain provisional; a weak association alone does not justify changing them.

6. What remains necessary

  • Confirm the monthly LST source metadata. The CSV is now available and its provisional audit is above. The extraction script must establish rainfall provenance, count semantics, cloud handling and monthly compositing.
  • Confirm the original geography. Trees have been assigned to current official Ortsteil polygons. The source polygons and Buckow component identifiers are needed to validate that alignment and resolve the excluded areas.
  • Refine response timing and vulnerability. Daily watering dates support shorter antecedent-weather windows, subject to timestamp and recording audits. Assess differential response using an explicitly specified model.
  • Assess cooling separately. Provisional same-month and next-month LST descriptions are now available. A causal cooling claim additionally requires a credible comparison design, exposure completeness and adequate detectability. No causal cooling estimate is produced here.
  • Keep municipal priorities conditional. Care-versus-need screening can proceed with independent need measures. An allocation that maximises cooling has not been established.
