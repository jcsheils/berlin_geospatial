# Related literature

## Scope

This review positions the thesis as the [refined October 2026 proposal](proposal/Proposal_Refined_October_2026.pdf) defined it: Part I on drought-related vegetation cooling loss, Part II on civic climate response, vulnerability and the spatial information in logged care, and Part III on allocation. The [November 2026 revision](revised_research_design_2026_11.md) keeps RQ1, turns RQ2 towards public levers and reach, replaces RQ3 with tree outcomes and widens RQ4 to policy scenarios. Its own reference list covers co-production, stewardship, young-tree survival and quasi-experimental methods; this review does not yet include them.

The implemented work covers tree-linked Gieß den Kiez logs for May 2020–December 2025 across 542 LOR Planungsräume, global and local Moran analysis of reach, comparison with MSS 2025 and the Environmental Justice Atlas 2023/24, monthly weather associations and the RQ1 daily pilot. All of it is descriptive. The data omit municipal and unlogged private watering, and watering-induced satellite cooling is outside the design.

## Core references

Five must-cites: Schwaab et al. (2021) for the European tree–LST baseline in Part I; Gobatti et al. (2025) for the soil-moisture and irrigation mechanism; Baró et al. (2019) as the distributional-justice anchor, where RQ2 moves the outcome from benefit stocks to care flows; Moskell et al. (2016) as the closest precedent on resident street-tree watering; and Lachmund (2022) for civic stewardship around Berlin tree pits. Egerer et al. (2024) is the sixth, because it names Gieß den Kiez as a Berlin coordination example. Roberts et al. (2017) and Openshaw (1984) cover spatial cross-validation and the modifiable areal unit problem.

## Annotated list

### Berlin heat exposure and vulnerability

1. **Scherer, D., Fehrenbach, U., Lakes, T., Lauf, S., Meier, F., and Schuster, C. (2014).** Quantification of heat-stress related mortality hazard, vulnerability and risk in Berlin, Germany. *DIE ERDE*, 144(3-4), 238-259. https://doi.org/10.12854/erde-144-17
   Combines Berlin heat hazard, vulnerability and mortality risk to argue for targeted adaptation. No tree care or volunteer behaviour.
2. **Schuster, C., Burkart, K., and Lakes, T. (2014).** Heat mortality in Berlin - Spatial variability at the neighborhood scale. *Urban Climate*, 10, 134-147. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.uclim.2014.10.008
   The closest Berlin precedent at neighbourhood scale; its outcome is heat mortality, not an adaptive action.
3. **Westerholt, R., and Klopfer, F. (2024).** Studying the spatial structure of urban heat exposure in Berlin utilising Moran drop plots and seismograms. *AGILE GIScience Series*, 5, 52. https://doi.org/10.5194/agile-giss-5-52-2024
   Applies spatial autocorrelation to Berlin heat; this project applies Moran statistics to inventory-normalised watering reach.
4. **Klopfer, F., and Pfeiffer, A. (2023).** Determining spatial disparities and similarities regarding heat exposure, green provision, and social structure of urban areas - A study on the city district level in the Ruhr area, Germany. *Heliyon*, 9(6), e16185. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2023.e16185
   Joins heat, green provision and social structure much as the equity analysis does, but measures conditions, not maintenance effort.

### Tree cooling, soil moisture and irrigation

5. **Schwaab, J., Meier, R., Mussetti, G., Seneviratne, S. I., Bürgi, C., and Davin, E. L. (2021).** The role of urban trees in reducing land surface temperatures in European cities. *Nature Communications*, 12, 6763. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-26768-w
   Establishes tree cooling across European cities; silent on who maintains trees.
6. **Gobatti, L., Bach, P. M., Maurer, M., et al. (2025).** Impact of soil moisture content on urban tree evaporative cooling and human thermal comfort. *npj Urban Sustainability*, 5, 26. https://doi.org/10.1038/s42949-025-00220-0
   Models irrigation scenarios in Zurich. A Berlin extension would use observed, tree-linked watering; the repository does not yet attempt that thermal analysis.
7. **Cuthbert, M. O., Rau, G. C., Ekström, M., O'Carroll, D. M., and Bates, A. J. (2022).** Global climate-driven trade-offs between the water retention and cooling benefits of urban greening. *Nature Communications*, 13, 518. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-28160-8
   Frames the hydrological trade-off behind irrigation cooling with global models; this project adds local evidence on where supplemental water is logged.
8. **McDonald, R. I., Chakraborty, T. C., Endreny, T. A., Parsons, L. A., Marsagishvili, M., and Esperon-Rodriguez, M. (2026).** Trees halve urban heat island effect globally but unequal benefits only modestly mitigate climate-change warming. *Nature Communications*, 17. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-71825-x
   Shows uneven tree cooling, often larger in cooler or wealthier places. Volunteer maintenance is one candidate mechanism.
9. **Su, Y., Makowski, D., Zhang, X., et al. (2026).** A remote sensing-based assessment of the cooling effects of urban trees in European cities. *npj Urban Sustainability*, 6, 96. https://doi.org/10.1038/s42949-026-00399-w
   Analyses mixed 100 m urban cells instead of pure tree and impervious surfaces. Its support-aware LST design is the method reference for Part I.

    Links income to forest provision. This project moves from the stock of urban forest to the flow of care.
11. **Baró, F., Calderón-Argelich, A., Langemeyer, J., and Connolly, J. J. T. (2019).** Under one canopy? Assessing the distributional environmental justice implications of street tree benefits in Barcelona. *Environmental Science & Policy*, 102, 54-64. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsci.2019.08.016

12. **Locke, D. H., Hall, B., Grove, J. M., et al. (2021).** Residential housing segregation and urban tree canopy in 37 US cities. *npj Urban Sustainability*, 1, 15. https://doi.org/10.1038/s42949-021-00022-0

## Positioning

The literature splits into four streams: Berlin studies of heat exposure, vulnerability and mortality; urban-climate studies of cooling from canopy and water; environmental-justice studies of unequal canopy and services; and stewardship studies of participation and care practice. This project connects the last two with behavioural data. It maps **where recorded citizen watering occurs**, normalises it by the inventoried trees that could have been watered, and compares it with social vulnerability and environmental burden.

Preliminary differentiators, all hypothesis-generating:

- **Action, not stock.** The outcome is logged watering, not canopy, greenness or modelled services.
- **Tree-linked denominators.** Events, litres and watered trees link to the cadastre and divide by local inventory, so tree-rich areas do not dominate raw counts.
- **Fine scale.** 542 Planungsräume reveal variation that Berlin's 12 Bezirke hide.
- **Explicit spatial dependence.** Global Moran's I for reach is 0.441 (permutation p = 0.001), followed by local clusters.
- **Two kinds of need.** Reach has a small negative association with child poverty (Spearman ρ = −0.123) and a strong positive association with the Environmental Justice Atlas burden count (ρ = 0.486).
- **Screening.** Ten Planungsräume combine low social status, thermal or green need and below-median reach. They are candidates for validation, not proven priorities, and their low counts do not prove an absence of unlogged watering.
- **Reproducibility.** Official spatial services, auditable joins, notebook builders, tables, maps and GeoPackages ship with the repository.

## Draft related-work paragraph

Prior Berlin research has mapped neighbourhood heat exposure, vulnerability and heat-related mortality (Scherer et al., 2014; Schuster et al., 2014; Westerholt and Klopfer, 2024). Urban-climate studies show that tree cooling depends on canopy, urban form and water availability (Schwaab et al., 2021; Cuthbert et al., 2022; Gobatti et al., 2025). Environmental-justice research finds urban forests and street-tree benefits unequally distributed (Gerrish and Watkins, 2018; Baró et al., 2019; Locke et al., 2021). A separate stewardship literature examines how community groups, outreach and civic participation shape tree care (Conway et al., 2011; Moskell et al., 2016; Lachmund, 2022). These literatures rarely measure the distribution of maintenance effort itself. This study links the May 2020–December 2025 Gieß den Kiez logs to Berlin's tree cadastre, normalises watering by local inventory, tests its spatial clustering across 542 Planungsräume and compares the patterns with official social and environmental-justice indicators.

## Claims to avoid

- A causal cooling effect of citizen watering. The results are descriptive.
- "Watering favours wealthy areas." Middle-status areas have the highest median reach, environmental burden associates positively with watering, and child poverty associates weakly and negatively.
- "First use of Gieß den Kiez." Published Berlin stewardship work already mentions the platform. The defensible claim is narrower: **a citywide, multi-year, inventory-normalised spatial equity analysis of its public logs**.
- Citing the heat-mortality paper as "Burkart et al." The author order is Schuster, Burkart and Lakes (2014).
